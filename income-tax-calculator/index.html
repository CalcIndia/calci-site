Common questions

Quick answers about the two tax regimes, slabs, rebate and deductions.

?Which tax regime is better for me?

It depends on your deductions. The new regime has lower rates but almost no deductions. The old regime has higher rates but allows 80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest and more. Enter your numbers above to see which one costs less.

?Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free?

Under the new regime, yes: the Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 makes taxable income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free. For salaried people, the ₹75,000 standard deduction takes this to ₹12.75 lakh of salary.

?What are the old regime slabs?

Up to ₹2.5 lakh: nil (₹3 lakh for ages 60 to 79, ₹5 lakh for 80 and above) · ₹2.5–5 lakh: 5% · ₹5–10 lakh: 20% · above ₹10 lakh: 30%. A rebate makes taxable income up to ₹5 lakh tax-free.

?What is the standard deduction?

A flat deduction for salaried people and pensioners: ₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime. You do not need any proof to claim it.

?Which deductions are allowed in the new regime?

Very few for most people: the standard deduction, your employer's NPS contribution and a few others. 80C, 80D, HRA and home loan interest on a self-occupied house are only available in the old regime.

?What is marginal relief?

If your income is just above ₹12 lakh in the new regime, the tax cannot be more than the income above ₹12 lakh. The same idea applies at the surcharge limits. This calculator includes both.

?Can I switch between regimes every year?

Salaried people can choose each year when filing their return. People with business income can switch only a limited number of times.

?Is this calculation final?

It is an estimate for resident individuals. It does not cover special-rate income such as capital gains, or every possible deduction. Check with a tax adviser before filing.
